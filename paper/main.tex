\begin{abstract}
Hybrid quantum--classical diffusion models for image generation are usually
compared against classical networks of similar size, which leaves open whether
the parameterized circuit itself contributes anything that a classical layer of
the same shape could not. We study this question in a setting where it is also
practically relevant: consistency distillation of a hybrid latent diffusion
teacher into a one-step student, which reduces the number of circuit
evaluations per sample by up to $32\times$. The models are a latent
denoiser with a 4--8-qubit variational branch added to a classical MLP, trained
on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, KMNIST and PneumoniaMNIST with three seeds. Besides
fully classical teachers and students, we use three controls that isolate
different parts of the circuit: an exact, linear-cost classical simulation of
the same circuit without entangling gates; a classical branch with the same
inputs, outputs and range; and an ablation that replaces the circuit's
input-dependent output with its mean. Distillation works: a one-step student
matches the 32-step teacher on all four datasets and, at an equal finite-shot
budget, beats the few-step teacher. The circuit does not contribute measurably.
Across 12 paired runs, hybrid and classical teachers differ by $-0.2\%$ in
classifier-feature \fidlike{} (90\% CI $[-1.5\%, +1.0\%]$), and hybrid students
and their separable surrogates by $-0.2\%$ ($[-0.5\%, 0.0\%]$); both are
equivalent within a $\pm10\%$ margin. In the trained models the branch acts
mostly as a learned constant: its input-dependent part is 5--11\% of the
backbone's output, and replacing it with its mean changes \fidlike{} by
0--3 points. In a preregistered capacity sweep and a circuit-scaling sweep
(up to 8 qubits, depth 4, with and without data re-uploading), the circuit
beat the size-matched classical control in 1 of 24 cells and entanglement beat
the separable surrogate in none; where the circuit carries the whole model, it
was often worse than the classical control. These are negative results for
one model family at small scale, under exact simulation and simple noise;
they are not claims about quantum circuits in general.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords:} quantum machine learning; consistency distillation; latent
diffusion; benchmarking; classical surrogates; negative results

\section{Introduction}

Diffusion models generate data by reversing a gradual noising process
\citep{ho2020ddpm}, at the cost of many sequential network evaluations.
Latent diffusion moves this process into the latent space of an autoencoder
\citep{rombach2022ldm}, and consistency models learn a map from any point of a
sampling trajectory directly to its clean endpoint, so that one or a few
evaluations suffice \citep{song2023consistency,luo2023lcm}.

The same two ideas appear in quantum generative modelling. Several recent
models place variational circuits inside the denoiser of a latent diffusion
model and report better image quality than classical networks with a similar
number of parameters \citep{defalco2024efficient,defalco2025qldm,yeteraydeniz2025medical,qian2026hybrid}.
Others reduce the number of circuit calls with consistency-style or unitary
single-step samplers \citep{kolle2024qddm,zhang2025oqiddm}. For a circuit on
near-term hardware the number of calls matters more than for a GPU network,
because every call costs a full set of measurement shots. Distilling a
hybrid teacher into a one-step student is therefore an appealing way to cut the
quantum cost of sampling.

Two questions are usually conflated in this literature. The first is whether
the hybrid model works; the second is whether it works \emph{because of the
circuit}. Parameter-matched classical baselines answer the first but not the
second, since they differ from the hybrid model in architecture, not only in
the circuit. Benchmarking studies of quantum classifiers have found that
simple classical models often match or beat them and that removing
entanglement often does not hurt \citep{bowles2024better}, and many
re-uploading models admit efficient classical surrogates \citep{schreiber2023surrogates}.
Whether this also holds for hybrid diffusion models has, to our knowledge,
not been tested with controls that isolate the circuit.

We study it in the distillation setting described above. Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A hybrid latent consistency-distillation pipeline in which both the
  teacher and the student can contain a variational branch, with explicit
  accounting of circuit evaluations, timing, and finite-shot cost
  (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A set of controls that isolate the circuit: an exact classical
  simulation of the same circuit without entangling gates, a size-matched
  classical branch, fully classical networks, and ablations that remove either
  the whole branch or only its input-dependent part (Section~\ref{sec:controls}).
  \item Evidence that distillation transfers across four datasets: the one-step
  student matches the 32-step teacher and, at equal shot budget, beats the
  few-step teacher (Sections~\ref{sec:res-distill} and~\ref{sec:res-shots}).
  \item Evidence, from equivalence tests and from preregistered sweeps over
  capacity, qubit count, depth and data re-uploading, that the circuit
  contributes nothing that the classical controls do not reproduce
  (Sections~\ref{sec:res-equivalence}--\ref{sec:res-sweeps}).
\end{enumerate}

We make no claim of quantum advantage, and our circuits (4--8 qubits) are
trivially simulable. The question is narrower and, we think, prior to any
advantage claim: whether, in this model family, the circuit changes the
function the model learns.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Diffusion, distillation and consistency models.}
DDIM turns a trained diffusion model into a deterministic sampler that can skip
steps \citep{song2021ddim}. Progressive distillation halves the number of steps
repeatedly and introduced the $v$-parameterisation we use for the teacher
\citep{salimans2022progressive}. Consistency distillation trains a student to
give the same output at adjacent points of the teacher's sampling trajectory,
with an exponential-moving-average target and a boundary condition at $t=0$
\citep{song2023consistency}; latent consistency models apply this in the latent
space of a pretrained latent diffusion model \citep{luo2023lcm}. We follow this
recipe in a small latent space and use a cosine noise schedule
\citep{nichol2021improved}.

\paragraph{Quantum and hybrid diffusion models.}
QuDDPM and its mixed-state extension define diffusion over quantum states
\citep{zhang2023quddpm,kwun2024mixedquddpm}. For classical images,
\citet{kolle2024qddm} propose quantum denoising diffusion models and a unitary
single-sampling variant inspired by consistency models;
\citet{zhang2025oqiddm} combine optimized quantum implicit neural networks with
consistency models for $O(1)$-step sampling (we could only access the abstract
and a partial preview of this work, so we do not compare against its
equations). In latent space, \citet{defalco2025qldm} use an autoencoder with a
10-dimensional latent and variational circuits for image, time and their
combination, \citet{yeteraydeniz2025medical} use quantum residual blocks for
medical images, and \citet{qian2026hybrid} run a mixed-state quantum diffusion
model in an autoencoder's latent space. These works compare against classical
models of similar size. None, to our knowledge, distils a hybrid teacher into a
few-step student, and none includes a control that keeps the architecture
fixed and removes only the circuit or only its entanglement.

\paragraph{Benchmarking and dequantisation.}
\citet{bowles2024better} benchmark twelve quantum classifiers against simple
classical models and find that the classical models usually win and that
removing entanglement often does not hurt. \citet{schreiber2023surrogates}
show that large classes of re-uploading models have efficiently computable
classical surrogates. The function computed by a circuit with angle encoding
is a truncated Fourier series in the inputs whose frequencies are set by the
encoding \citep{schuld2021encoding}; re-uploading raises the available
frequencies \citep{perezsalinas2020reuploading}. Our separable surrogate is an
instance of an exact classical surrogate: without entangling gates, the circuit
factorises into independent single-qubit rotations of Bloch vectors.

\subsection{Latent space and noise schedule}

A convolutional autoencoder (two stride-2 convolutions, a linear layer to a
16-dimensional latent, and the mirror decoder) is trained for 20 epochs with a
pixel MSE loss on 10{,}000 training images (4{,}708 for PneumoniaMNIST, its full
training split). Latents are standardised with the per-dimension training mean
and standard deviation. We use $T=32$ discrete noise levels with a cosine
schedule \citep{nichol2021improved},
\begin{equation}
  \abar_t = \max\!\left(\cos^2\!\frac{\pi t}{2T},\,10^{-6}\right),\quad \abar_0=1,\qquad
  z_t=\sqrt{\abar_t}\,z_0+\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,\epsilon,\ \ \epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),
\end{equation}
so that $z_T$ is essentially pure noise and sampling starts from $\mathcal{N}(0,I)$.

\subsection{Network and branch}
\label{sec:network}

Every teacher and student uses the network of Figure~\ref{fig:arch}. With $u=t/T$, the
input features are $\phi=[z_t,\,u,\,\sin\pi u,\,\cos\pi u]$. The output
is the sum of a classical backbone (a three-layer MLP with SiLU activations
and hidden width $h$; $h=128$ unless stated) and a branch
\begin{equation}
  B(\phi)=W_r\,g\!\left(\pi\tanh(W_c\phi+b_c)\right)+b_r,
\end{equation}
where the compressor produces $2q$ angles $(\theta,\varphi)$ for $q$ qubits,
$g:\mathbb{R}^{2q}\to[-1,1]^q$ is the branch block, and $(W_r,b_r)$ is a linear
readout to the 16-dimensional latent. With $h=128$ and $q=4$, the branch has 256
of the network's 21{,}392 parameters.

\paragraph{Circuit.} The hybrid branch block prepares the product state
$\bigotimes_j R_Z(\varphi_j)R_Y(\theta_j)|0\rangle$, applies $L$ trainable layers
$U_\ell=\mathrm{CZ}_{\mathrm{ring}}\bigotimes_j R_Z(w_{\ell j2})R_Y(w_{\ell j1})$,
where $\mathrm{CZ}_{\mathrm{ring}}$ applies CZ between neighbouring qubits on a
ring, then a fixed $\bigotimes_j R_Y(\pi/4)$, and returns the exact expectation
values $\langle Z_j\rangle$. The final rotation makes phases and entanglement
visible to a $Z$ readout; without it, the last layer's $R_Z$ and CZ gates would
not affect the output. The circuit has $2qL$ trainable parameters (16 for
$q=4$, $L=2$). With data re-uploading, the encoding rotations are repeated
before every trainable layer. All circuits are simulated exactly as state
vectors; the data-independent gates of each forward pass are fused into one
matrix, which is tested against gate-by-gate simulation.

\subsection{Teacher}

The teacher predicts $v=\sqrt{\abar_t}\,\epsilon-\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,z_0$
\citep{salimans2022progressive} and is trained with an MSE loss for 150 epochs
(AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019adamw}, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 64). It
samples with deterministic DDIM \citep{song2021ddim} on $k$ uniformly spaced
levels between $T$ and $1$, followed by a final step to $t=0$.

\subsection{Consistency distillation}

The student wraps the network in a parameterisation that enforces the
boundary condition $f_\theta(z,0)=z$:
\begin{equation}
  f_\theta(z_t,t)=\abar_t\,z_t+\sqrt{1-\abar_t}\,F_\theta(z_t,t).
  \label{eq:student}
\end{equation}
For a clean latent $z_0$, a level $t\sim\mathcal{U}\{1,\dots,T\}$ and noise
$\epsilon$, let $\hat z_{t-1}=\Phi(z_t;t\to t-1)$ be one DDIM step of the frozen
teacher. The loss is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(\theta)=\mathbb{E}\left\|f_\theta(z_t,t)-f_{\theta^-}(\hat z_{t-1},t-1)\right\|_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\theta^-$ is an exponential moving average of $\theta$ with decay
$\mu$, updated after every optimiser step \citep{song2023consistency}. Unless
stated otherwise, students train for 200 epochs with $\mu=0.95$
(Section~\ref{sec:res-budget} explains this choice). Students of different
kinds trained from the same teacher share the initialisation seed and the exact
sequence of minibatches and noise draws. One-step sampling returns
$f_\theta(z_T,T)$. With $k$ steps, the student's prediction at each level is
re-noised to the next level with fresh noise before the next evaluation.

\subsection{Controls}
\label{sec:controls}

Each control changes one thing about the network of Section~\ref{sec:network}
and leaves everything else (data, autoencoder, teacher, seeds, minibatch order)
fixed.
\begin{description}
  \item[No entanglement.] The same circuit without CZ gates.
  \item[Separable surrogate.] An exact classical implementation of the
  no-entanglement circuit: each qubit is a Bloch vector rotated by the same
  $R_Y$ and $R_Z$ angles, and the output is $(b_z-b_x)/\sqrt2$, which equals
  $\langle Z\rangle$ after the final $R_Y(\pi/4)$. It has the same parameters and
  gradients as the no-entanglement circuit, at cost linear in $q$. Unit tests
  confirm that it matches the no-entanglement circuit and differs from the
  entangled one on random inputs.
  \item[Classical branch.] The branch block is replaced by
  $\tanh(\mathrm{Linear}(2q\to q))$: same input, same output dimension and range,
  with cross-feature interactions (36 parameters for $q=4$, against the
  circuit's 16, which favours the control).
  \item[Classical.] No branch at all; only the backbone.
  \item[Branch ablations at evaluation.] In a trained model, either zero the
  readout (removing the branch, including its learned bias), or replace the
  circuit output $g$ by its mean over noisy training latents
  (\texttt{features\_mean}), which removes only the input-dependent part of
  the branch.
\end{description}

\subsection{Cost accounting}

Each sampling step evaluates the network once per sample, so a $k$-step
sampler with a circuit makes $k$ circuit evaluations per sample. We report
end-to-end wall-clock time for 512 samples, including the decoder, as the median
of five synchronised repetitions after two warm-up runs on one RTX~3080~Ti.
Simulated circuit time is not a proxy for hardware latency; for hardware cost
we report total measurement shots per sample (steps $\times$ shots per
evaluation) in Section~\ref{sec:res-shots}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Datasets.} MNIST \citep{lecun1998mnist}, Fashion-MNIST
\citep{xiao2017fashion}, KMNIST \citep{clanuwat2018kmnist} and PneumoniaMNIST
from MedMNIST v2 \citep{yang2023medmnist} (two unbalanced classes,
1{,}214/3{,}494 training images; CC BY 4.0), all $28\times28$ greyscale.

\paragraph{Metric.} Following \citet{heusel2017fid}, we compute the Fréchet
distance between Gaussian fits to features of 512 generated samples and 1{,}000
real test images, but the features are the 64-dimensional penultimate layer of
a small CNN classifier trained separately for each dataset, not Inception. We
call this score \fidlike{}; lower is better. It is only comparable within a
dataset in this paper, never across datasets or to published FID values. The
classifiers were trained with budgets fixed before inspecting the test split
and reach held-out accuracies of 0.959 (MNIST), 0.889 (Fashion-MNIST), 0.931
(KMNIST) and 0.827 (PneumoniaMNIST). We also record the entropy of the
classifier's predicted labels as a diversity check.

\paragraph{Teacher gate.} Before training students, each 32-step teacher had
to reach a label entropy of at least $0.8\ln 10$ (for PneumoniaMNIST, $0.8$
times the entropy of the training labels) and a mean classifier confidence of at
least 0.8 times that on real images. All 36 teachers of the dataset campaign
passed.

\paragraph{Seeds and runs.} Every configuration uses seeds 0, 1, 2; the seed
fixes the autoencoder, teacher and student, so each seed is an independent
replicate of the whole pipeline. Paired comparisons match seeds.

\paragraph{Experiments.} The work proceeded in four stages, each fixed before
it was run:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Pilot} (MNIST, hybrid teacher; students with 50 epochs, $\mu=0.999$),
  followed by a student-budget diagnostic that reused the pilot's autoencoders and
  teachers and varied only epochs and $\mu$ (Section~\ref{sec:res-budget}).
  \item \emph{Dataset and teacher campaign}: 4 datasets $\times$ 3 seeds; on each
  autoencoder, a hybrid, a classical and a no-entanglement teacher. The hybrid
  teacher is distilled into hybrid, classical and surrogate students; the
  control teachers into a classical student
  (Sections~\ref{sec:res-distill}--\ref{sec:res-equivalence}).
  \item \emph{Preregistered extension} \textbf{E0--E4}, with criteria written
  into the repository before any run (Sections~\ref{sec:res-e0}--\ref{sec:res-e4}):
  E0, branch ablations of the campaign models; E1, a capacity sweep over $h$;
  E2, circuit scaling with no backbone; E3, finite shots and noise at
  inference; E4, diagnostics of multi-step degradation and of KMNIST.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Preregistered criteria (E1, E2).} In each cell (dataset, $h$, or
circuit configuration), with teacher and student of the same kind and size and
the one-step student's \fidlike{} as the primary metric: the \emph{circuit
contributes} if the hybrid model beats the classical branch in all three seeds
and its mean is at least 10\% lower; \emph{entanglement contributes} if the
hybrid beats the separable surrogate under the same rule. The 32-step teacher
and the 4-step student are secondary metrics.

\paragraph{Equivalence analysis (post hoc).} The criteria above can detect a
contribution but cannot establish its absence. We therefore add, after the
fact, two one-sided tests (TOST) on paired log-ratios of \fidlike{}
\citep{lakens2017tost}. We report the mean ratio and its 90\% $t$-interval
and call two models \emph{equivalent} if the interval lies inside
$[1/1.1,\,1.1]$, the same 10\% used by the preregistered criteria. Pooled tests
treat the 12 dataset--seed pairs as independent replicates. With three seeds
per cell, single-cell intervals are wide; we show them in Figure~\ref{fig:forest}
but base equivalence claims only on pooled tests.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Student budget: the pilot was undertrained}
\label{sec:res-budget}

In the pilot, no student met the advance criterion we had proposed (in every
seed, \fidlike{} at most 10\% worse than the 32-step teacher, label entropy loss
at most $0.1\ln10$, and at least $2\times$ faster): the best one-step students
scored 347.5 (hybrid) and 426.9 (classical), against 24.7 for the 32-step
teacher (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). A diagnostic that changed only the student's
epochs and EMA decay showed that the students, not distillation, were the
problem. With $\mu=0.999$ and 50 epochs the target network lags too far behind
to propagate the boundary condition up to $t=T$. Lowering $\mu$ to 0.95, or
training four times longer, brings the one-step \fidlike{} to 28--32. With
200 epochs and $\mu=0.95$, four-step hybrid, classical and surrogate students
meet the advance criterion in all three seeds. We use this budget for all later
students. Already here, hybrid and classical students are indistinguishable.

\subsection{Distillation transfers across datasets}
\label{sec:res-distill}

Figure~\ref{fig:steps} and Table~\ref{tab:campaign} show the dataset campaign.
On all four datasets, the one-step student is at the level of the 32-step
teacher and far ahead of the teacher with one to four DDIM steps. Pooled over
datasets and seeds, the one-step hybrid student's \fidlike{} is $1.7\%$
lower than the 32-step teacher's (90\% CI $[-8.0\%, +5.0\%]$). On MNIST
this means 28.0 in about 11~ms for 512 samples, against 24.7 in 328~ms for the
32-step teacher and 31.1 for its 8-step version. The advance criterion is met in all
seeds and datasets by the students of the hybrid teacher (at 4 steps on MNIST,
1--8 steps on Fashion-MNIST and KMNIST, and 1--2 steps on PneumoniaMNIST).

Students do not improve monotonically with steps. On MNIST and especially
PneumoniaMNIST, quality degrades beyond 4--8 steps (\fidlike{} $\approx100$ at 32
steps on PneumoniaMNIST, with large seed variance); on KMNIST it improves.
Section~\ref{sec:res-e4} traces this to a fraction of diverging samples.
KMNIST has high absolute \fidlike{} for every model; Section~\ref{sec:res-e4}
attributes this to the autoencoder.

\subsection{Hybrid and classical models are equivalent}
\label{sec:res-equivalence}

branch have indistinguishable DDIM curves on all datasets
(Table~\ref{tab:campaign}). The same holds for the students of the hybrid
teacher (Table~\ref{tab:students}). Table~\ref{tab:equivalence} turns this into

\subsection{E0: the branch is mostly a learned constant}
\label{sec:res-e0}

The branch is not ignored by the optimiser: zeroing its readout raises the
teacher's \fidlike{} by 19--100\% depending on the dataset, and for the
PneumoniaMNIST student from 5.8 to 22.2 (Table~\ref{tab:e0}). But almost all of
this effect comes from a constant. The input-dependent part of the branch has
5--11\% of the backbone's RMS output (median over $t$), and replacing it with
its mean changes \fidlike{} by 0--3 points. In these models, the branch adds
a learned offset to the backbone's output, and the circuit's input dependence
barely matters. This also explains the insensitivity to shots and noise in
Section~\ref{sec:res-shots}.

\subsection{E1 and E2: no regime in which the circuit contributes}
\label{sec:res-sweeps}

E0 suggests that the large backbone leaves the branch nothing to do. E1
therefore shrinks the backbone, down to removing it ($h=0$), so that the branch
carries more or all of the model (Figure~\ref{fig:capacity},
Table~\ref{tab:e1}). E2 then scales the circuit itself with no backbone: 4, 6
and 8 qubits, depth 2 and 4, with and without data re-uploading
(Table~\ref{tab:e2}).

\paragraph{Preregistered criteria.} On the primary metric, the circuit
contributes in 1 of 24 cells and entanglement in 0 of 24. The single pass is
MNIST with $h=16$ (hybrid 11\% better than the classical branch). There, the
separable surrogate is equal to the hybrid in every seed (35.2/30.4/46.2
against 35.2/30.5/44.0), the backbone-only MLP is within noise, and the effect
reverses on Fashion-MNIST. So the advantage belongs to the separable function,
which is classically simulable, and is isolated. On the secondary metrics (four-step
student, 32-step teacher), E1 has 5 passes in 48 tests (4 for the circuit, 1 for
entanglement), scattered with no pattern in $h$ or dataset and uncorrected for
multiple comparisons; in E2 the circuit criterion passes only for the teacher at
4 qubits and depth 4 (with and without re-uploading), where the separable surrogate
equals the hybrid, and the entanglement criterion never passes.

\paragraph{When the circuit carries the model, it tends to hurt.} With no or a
tiny backbone on Fashion-MNIST, the classical branch is much better than the
circuit (ratio 1.54 at $h=0$ and 1.31 at $h=4$). In E2, data re-uploading makes
every one-step hybrid student worse (ratios 1.43--2.86 against the classical
branch) and much more variable across seeds, with no consistent effect on the
teacher. Without re-uploading, more qubits help (one-step \fidlike{} 144, 86, 52
for 4, 6, 8 qubits at depth 2), but a classical branch of the same width
improves the same way (139, 73, 55). The gain comes from the width of the
compressor and readout ($2q$ inputs, $q$ outputs), not from the circuit.

\paragraph{Capacity frontier.} From $h=16$ upward ($h=8$ on MNIST), hybrid models and
backbone-only MLPs with comparable parameter counts coincide
(Figure~\ref{fig:capacity}). At $h=0$--4, the hybrid beats a backbone-only MLP
of similar size, but so does the classical branch: the advantage belongs to
the compressor--branch--readout structure, not to the circuit.

\paragraph{Distillation needs a backbone.} With $h=0$, distillation fails: the
one-step students score 131--157 (99 for the Fashion-MNIST classical branch)
against 52--57 for the 32-step teachers. Without
a backbone, the student lacks the capacity for the one-step map. With 8 qubits
and no re-uploading the gap narrows (52.1 against 46.3), and the classical branch
does the same (55.3).

\paragraph{Per-cell intervals.} Figure~\ref{fig:forest} shows the hybrid/control
ratio with 90\% intervals for all 48 primary comparisons. With three seeds,
only 9 cells are individually equivalent within $\pm10\%$; most intervals
straddle both 1 and the margin. The sweep does not prove equivalence in every
cell. What it shows is that the 24 point estimates scatter around 1 for the
surrogate, and around or above 1 for the classical branch.

\subsection{E3: shots and noise}
\label{sec:res-shots}

We evaluated noiselessly trained MNIST models with finite shots and local
depolarising noise at inference. Each circuit evaluation is simulated as a
density matrix with depolarising probability $p$ on every qubit after the
encoding and after each trainable layer; all qubits are measured in the
computational basis with $S$ shots per evaluation, and $\langle Z_j\rangle$ are
estimated from the counts. Table~\ref{tab:e3} and Figure~\ref{fig:shots} show
$p\in\{0,0.03\}$ and $S\in\{\text{exact},64\}$; the full grid
($p\in\{0,0.001,0.01,0.03\}$, $S\in\{\text{exact},1024,256,64\}$) is in the
repository.

With the backbone ($h=128$), 64 shots and $p=0.03$ leave \fidlike{} unchanged.
This is what E0 predicts: the input-dependent part of the circuit hardly
matters. At an equal total shot budget, the distilled student wins: one step
with 64 shots (64 shots per sample) gives 27.9, against 52.2 for the
four-step teacher (256 shots per sample); the 32-step teacher needs 2{,}048
shots per sample to reach 24.7. Distillation therefore does reduce quantum cost,
but for a circuit that, in this model, could be removed. Without the backbone,
distillation fails (Section~\ref{sec:res-sweeps}) and the teacher wins at every
budget. Noise shifts the student by small amounts in both directions (sometimes
improving \fidlike{}, likely by adding variance), which is not evidence of
robustness.

\subsection{E4: why students degrade with steps, and the KMNIST bottleneck}
\label{sec:res-e4}

\paragraph{Multi-step degradation (E4a).} Measured against the endpoint of the
teacher's full DDIM trajectory from the same $z_t$, the student's error is small
at every $t$ (latent MSE 0.0003 at $t=1$ and 0.028 at $t=32$ on MNIST, against a
signal energy of about 1). The error is the same when the input is the
student's own re-noised output, so per-step error does not explain the
degradation. Instead, a growing fraction of samples diverges when the student is
applied repeatedly (Table~\ref{tab:e4a}): the median latent energy stays near 1,
but the tail explodes (maxima of $10^3$--$10^5$ at 32 steps), consistent with
the growing pixel variance we observed. Clipping or rescaling the latent
between steps, or training on the student's own re-noised predictions, are
possible fixes that we did not test. Hybrid and surrogate students behave the
same.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the controls rule out.} The equivalence of hybrid and classical
models could have had several explanations: the optimiser could ignore the
branch, the circuit could compute something useful that a classical branch also
computes, or entanglement could matter but be too weak to detect. The controls
separate these. The branch is used (zeroing it hurts) but mainly as a constant
(E0). A classical layer of the same shape matches or beats the circuit, so what
the circuit computes is reproducible classically (E1, E2). The exact separable
surrogate matches the entangled circuit in every pooled comparison and every
preregistered cell, so entanglement does not change the learned function in
these models.

The last result is plausible from the structure of the model. The compressor
maps a 19-dimensional input to $2q$ angles, each used once (without re-uploading),
so each $\langle Z_j\rangle$ is a low-degree trigonometric polynomial in those
angles \citep{schuld2021encoding}, followed by a learned linear readout. A
compressor and readout that are trained jointly with the circuit can absorb much
of what the circuit's entangling layers would add. Re-uploading raises the
available frequencies \citep{perezsalinas2020reuploading} but, here, mainly
made optimisation harder.

\paragraph{What remains.} Consistency distillation of a hybrid teacher is
effective and reduces circuit evaluations by $32\times$ at equal quality. At equal
shot budget it is better than shortening the teacher's DDIM schedule. This would
matter for any model in which the circuit could not be replaced. In the model
family studied here, it can be.

\paragraph{Implications for benchmarking.} Hybrid generative models are often
compared to classical models with a similar number of parameters. In our sweep,
this comparison favoured the hybrid model at small $h$, while a classical branch
of the same shape showed that the circuit was not the reason. We suggest that
claims of a benefit from a circuit should be accompanied by (i) a classical
block with the same inputs and outputs in the same place, (ii) an exact
separable surrogate when entanglement is claimed to matter, and (iii) an
ablation that replaces the circuit's output by its mean, which distinguishes a
learned bias from an input-dependent computation. All three are cheap.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The results hold for one architecture: an additive
branch on a classical MLP in a 16-dimensional autoencoder latent, with 4--8
qubits, depth up to 4, angle encoding and a hardware-efficient ansatz. Other
placements, encodings or ans\"atze could behave differently. Each cell has three
seeds and 512 samples; per-cell intervals are wide and the pooled tests treat
dataset--seed pairs as independent. The equivalence analysis and its margin were
chosen after the preregistered runs, although the margin equals the
preregistered threshold. There is no correction for multiple comparisons in
the per-cell criteria. \fidlike{} uses features of a small classifier and is
not comparable to standard FID. Noise was applied only at inference, with a
simple local depolarising model and no readout error or crosstalk; training
with shots or noise was not tested. We could not read the full text of the
closest consistency-based quantum diffusion model \citep{zhang2025oqiddm}.
Finally, our circuits are small enough to simulate exactly, so nothing here
bears on regimes where simulation is hard.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and history.} An earlier version of this project
(\texttt{v1/} in the repository) used a classical teacher and an earlier
training procedure. It contained implementation errors, documented in the
repository, and its raw outputs could not be fully recovered; none of its
numbers are used here. Every number in this paper is regenerated from the
committed JSON reports by \texttt{paper/analysis/make\_assets.py}; training and
evaluation code, configurations, the preregistration and environment records
are in \texttt{v2/}.

\section{Conclusion}

We distilled hybrid quantum latent diffusion teachers into one-step consistency
students and asked, with controls that isolate the circuit, whether the circuit
contributes. Distillation transferred across four datasets: the one-step
student matched the 32-step teacher, reduced circuit evaluations by $32\times$,
and won at equal shot budget. The circuit did not contribute: hybrid,
no-entanglement and classical models were equivalent within $\pm10\%$ in pooled
tests, the trained branch acted mostly as a learned constant, and across 24
preregistered capacity and scaling cells no regime showed a contribution that a
size-matched classical control or an exact separable surrogate did not
reproduce. We see this as a negative result for one model family and as a case
for including such controls in hybrid generative benchmarks. Natural next steps
are architectures in which the circuit cannot be bypassed, such as models of
quantum data \citep{zhang2023quddpm}, where distilling a few-step student would
reduce a cost that cannot be avoided classically.
